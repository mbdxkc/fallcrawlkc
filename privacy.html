    Privacy

    This site does not collect anything about you.

    What we do not do

    No cookies. No analytics. No tracking pixels. No advertising tags. No fonts or scripts are loaded from another company. The one outside request is the weather check described below.

    Two small notes are kept in your browser while the tab is open: that you answered the 21+ question, and the last weather check. They stay on your device and are gone when you close the tab.

    The weather

    The moon in the header follows the real sky over Kansas City, and to dim it on cloudy nights the page asks Open-Meteo, a free weather service, for the current conditions in Kansas City. It asks about Kansas City, never about where you are. Your browser connects to Open-Meteo directly to do that, so, like any website, Open-Meteo sees your IP address and browser type. At most once every 30 minutes; nothing else is sent. The moon's position and phase are worked out on your own device.

    There are no forms. There is nothing to sign up for and nowhere to enter your name, email or phone number.

    Hosting

    The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web server, GitHub receives what your browser sends when it asks for a page: your IP address, your browser type and the page requested. GitHub uses that to serve the site and to protect it from abuse. That happens before anything on this page runs. We do not see it, and we do not keep it. It is covered by GitHub’s privacy statement.

    Links away from here

    This page links out to Instagram, to Google Maps for directions, to Google Calendar when the date is tapped on an Android phone, and to the websites and phone numbers of participating businesses and partners, and to mediabrilliance.io, the studio that built the site. Everywhere else, tapping the date makes a calendar file on your own device and sends nothing anywhere. Once you follow one of those you are on someone else’s service, under their rules and their privacy policy, not this one.

    Following or tagging us on Instagram happens entirely on Instagram. We see what any account sees, and nothing more.

    If this changes

    If the site ever starts collecting something, an email signup for instance, this page is updated in the same change that adds it, and the date below moves.
